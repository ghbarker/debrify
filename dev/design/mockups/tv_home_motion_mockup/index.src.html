Home screen · volume II · motion concepts

Home in motion

Eight layouts already ship — Canvas, Classic, Spotlight, Atrium, Mosaic, Promenade, Deck, Tonight. They differ in where things sit. Every one of them moves the same way: art cross-dissolves, a card rises 1.055, a rail glides. That's why a ninth arrangement of the same parts wouldn't feel like more.

These five are organised around a verb instead of a shape. Each one picks a single motion idea — pan, bloom, glide, parallax, zoom — and lets the layout follow from it. They're drawn at true TV scale (1920×1080 = 2× the app's logical pixels) with the real 116px ghost rail, and they're live: click a screen and drive it with the arrow keys. Atlas also answers Enter and Backspace.

The constraint every one of these has to survive

16.7 ms — one vsync 50 ms — measured home-board scrub, Mi Box 4

The Mi Box baseline is 50 ms median per frame and 88% missed vsync, and the cost is per keypress, not per second — each deliberate press re-runs the whole hero pipeline. So every concept below is judged on one question: does its motion add work per press, or does it move pixels that were already going to be painted? Transform and opacity are free-ish. Blur, saveLayer, and a second decode are not.

Trailer holeThe underlay video sits behind a translucent Flutter view. Nothing with Opacity or a filter may sit over the hole — so a concept animates the hole's rect, never its alpha.
Decode at card sizeEvery image is decoded at the size it paints, and neighbours are pre-decoded. A concept that shows more art at once must show it smaller.
One verbEach screen commits to a single motion. Layouts that animate three things at once read as busy on a 10-foot screen, and cost three times as much.
VI

Mural

Verb · pan

Nothing cross-dissolves. The wall is continuous, and moving through the rail walks the camera along it.

Live · arrows

←→ walk the wall · ↑↓ change rail — the wall repaints, the camera keeps its place

The motion

620 ms wall pan, cubic(.16,.84,.24,1) 200 ms rail glide — 6.4× faster than the wall 340 ms identity, masked slide-up

Backdrops are hung edge to edge with a 150px overlap and a soft mask on each seam, so the joins read as one painted surface. The rail moves at its own pitch, the wall at 1280px per title — the parallax is a consequence of the two speeds, not a separate effect.

Why it feels different

A cross-dissolve says "new screen". A pan says "same room, different corner" — the reason a gallery feels bigger than a slideshow.

It also fixes the thing Canvas can't hide: on a slow box a dissolve stalls mid-way and looks broken. A pan that arrives late just arrives late.

What it costs

Three panels live at a time — prev/cur/next in a recycled window. One decode per press, the same as today, and the pan is one Transform.translate on a RepaintBoundary.

Trailer hole is a fixed rect in the upper right; the wall pans behind it.

No new per-press workReuses the Canvas art layer + railsWants landscape stills; posters look wrong hung edge to edge
VII

Bloom

Verb · grow

The stage isn't a backdrop that changes. It's the card you're standing on, opened up.

Live · arrows

←→ along the shelf — watch where the picture comes from · ↑↓ change shelf

The motion

180 ms collapse back into the card 220 ms focus rest before it re-opens 420 ms bloom out, cubic(.2,.8,.2,1)

One art layer, permanently full-bleed, with its transform origin parked on the focused card's centre. Collapsed it's scale(cardW/1920) sitting exactly in the card's rect; open it's scale(1). The card leaves a dashed socket so the eye knows what left.

Why it feels different

Every other layout asks you to believe two unrelated things changed together. Bloom shows the causal link — this picture is that card — which is the same trick a good app transition plays when a thumbnail opens into a page.

What it costs

Genuinely cheap: no second image, no blur, no clip. A uniform scale on one already-decoded backdrop, which is why it survives a Mali-450.

The trailer hole animates with the bloom — it's an AnimatedPositioned rect, never a fading layer, so the punch-through stays legal.

Cheapest new motion hereShips the "where did that come from" momentFast scrubbing must cancel mid-bloom, not queue
VIII

Reel

Verb · glide

One title owns the whole screen. Down is the next title; right is a different mood entirely.

Live · arrows

↑↓ next title, full screen · ←→ switch lane — Continue, Popular, Because you watched

The motion

520 ms vertical glide, one screen height 0.93 rest scale on the outgoing panel 2.4 s dwell, then the trailer takes the frame

The column is 1080px per title. The panel you leave shrinks and dims as it goes, the one arriving scales up into place — so the movement has a direction and a weight instead of a fade.

Why it feels different

It's the only concept where a title is never a thumbnail. On a rail, a show is a 160px poster competing with eleven others; here it gets the whole 65-inch panel, its logo, its rating, and 2.4 seconds before the trailer starts.

Best home for Continue Watching, where the list is short and the decision is already half made.

What it costs

The least of the five. Two full-screen images exist at a time and only one is fully painted. No rail, no grid, no twelve simultaneous decodes.

Trailer hole is the whole panel and it's stationary — start and stop have nothing to move.

Lowest GPU + memory costThe one built for weak boxesBrowsing density: one title per screen
IX

Diorama

Verb · parallax

Three planes at three speeds, and cards that turn to face you. The screen gets a depth it doesn't actually have.

Live · arrows

←→ along the shelf — the back wall drifts, the shelf runs, the cards turn · ↑↓ change shelf

The motion

−0.06× back wall (drifts against you) 0.22× identity plane 1.00× shelf ±7° card yaw by distance from centre

Cards sit under one shared perspective, so the ones off to the side turn away and the focused one squares up to the viewer. A slow specular sweep crosses the back wall on an 11s loop — the only thing on the screen that moves when you don't.

Why it feels different

Flat art on a flat panel is what every app does. Depth is what a hardware product does — the reason an Apple TV screensaver reads as expensive and a wallpaper doesn't.

It also makes focus unmistakable from across a room: the focused card is the only one facing you.

What it costs

The most here — spend it knowingly. The yaw is a matrix per card, not a repaint, so it's affordable; the sweep is a translated gradient, never a blur or a shader.

Rule if it ships: on a GLES2 box the sweep is off and the yaw drops to the focused card only. The parallax stays — it's three transforms.

Most "designed" screenNeeds a TV-class gateThree moving planes = three chances to jank
X

Atlas

Verb · zoom

Navigation stops being scrolling and becomes a camera. Back pulls up to the map; OK dives into a row.

Live · arrows

Enter dive into the row · Backspace pull up to the map · ←→↑↓ move in either altitude

The motion

0.52× map altitude — every row on one screen 1.00× row altitude — hero art, full rail 500 ms camera, one transform on the whole board

The board is built once and never rebuilt; zooming is a single scale-and-translate on it. Rows you aren't in drop back to 12% so the row you're in is unmistakable at either altitude.

Why it feels different

It gives Home the one thing it has never had: an overview. Ten rows and eighty titles in a single glance, then a dive into the one you meant — instead of pressing down eleven times to find out what's below the fold.

BACK finally means something on Home too. Today it exits; here it pulls up.

What it costs

Scaling a large tree is a raster cost, so the board goes in one RepaintBoundary and posters at map altitude decode at map size (≈70px wide) — the same decode-at-paint-size rule, applied to two altitudes.

No trailer at map altitude; it resumes on the dive.

Only layout with an overviewGives BACK a job on HomeTwo altitudes = two decode sizes to manage

Which one, and why

All five are additive — a new value in tv_home_style, a new branch in the board build, nothing removed. Canvas stays the default until you say otherwise.

My pick: build Bloom first, then Mural. Bloom is the cheapest motion in the deck and the most distinctive — it changes the feel of a press without changing what's on screen, so it can drop into Canvas's own layout as a motion option rather than a whole new board. Mural follows naturally: same art layer, same rails, and it retires the cross-dissolve that stalls on the slow boxes we already know about.

Reel is the sleeper. It's the only concept whose cost goes down as the screen gets more ambitious, and it's the right answer for a Continue-Watching-first home on hardware that can't afford a board at all. Diorama is the showpiece and needs a GLES3 gate. Atlas is the biggest build and the only one that adds something we can't do today — worth it if the map is a feature you want, not just a transition.
